# European Commission Releases EU Taxonomy FAQs
Dec 2
Written By [The ESG Institute](https://www.the-esg-institute.org/blog?author=6495d387bc6f46275ebd3a5d)
![](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/642e883205cdec341bcebf38/48fc6e14-8f47-448a-89e5-afe8944c7f77/NEWS+The+ESG+Institute+European+Commission+Releases+EU+Taxonomy+FAQs.png)
**The European Commission’s newly released FAQs on the EU Taxonomy provide crucial guidance on sustainable finance, simplifying compliance with technical screening criteria, DNSH requirements, and CSRD alignment. Tailored insights for sectors and enhanced reporting clarity empower businesses, investors, and policymakers to align activities with Europe’s ambitious environmental goals.**

#### Why is This Important?
The EU Taxonomy is a pivotal tool in Europe’s sustainable finance strategy. It provides a clear framework to classify and report on activities contributing to environmental objectives, ensuring transparency and preventing greenwashing.
However, the complexity of its technical screening criteria (TSC), DNSH requirements, and alignment with other reporting frameworks, such as the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), has posed challenges for many organizations. The newly published FAQs aim to address these concerns by providing detailed guidance and removing ambiguities.
#### Key Takeaways from the FAQs
**1. Aligning with NACE Codes:**
The FAQs provide valuable insights into the interplay between the Taxonomy’s activity descriptions and the NACE (Statistical Classification of Economic Activities in the European Community) codes. While NACE codes offer a useful framework for categorizing economic activities, the European Commission emphasizes that these are indicative rather than prescriptive. The true determinant of an activity’s eligibility under the Taxonomy is its specific description in the Delegated Acts.
This clarification ensures that the Taxonomy’s classification system remains flexible and nuanced, allowing it to capture the diverse nature of economic activities without being overly constrained by rigid coding systems. For example, an activity might not fall neatly within a single NACE code but could still align with the Taxonomy based on its environmental contributions and adherence to technical screening criteria (TSC). This approach underscores the need for stakeholders to prioritize the detailed activity descriptions when assessing Taxonomy alignment, rather than relying solely on NACE codes as a binary measure of eligibility.
**2. Integration with ESRS and CSRD:**
The FAQs bridge the gap between the EU Taxonomy and broader sustainability reporting frameworks, particularly the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) and the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD). The ESRS provides a comprehensive set of standards for companies to report on sustainability impacts, including environmental, social, and governance (ESG) factors, while the CSRD establishes the legal framework for mandatory reporting by large companies.
The FAQs clarify that while the ESRS focuses on disclosing a company’s overall sustainability impacts, the EU Taxonomy takes a more targeted approach, classifying specific economic activities as environmentally sustainable based on defined criteria. This distinction is crucial for businesses aiming to align their reporting efforts, ensuring that their disclosures under the ESRS can complement, but not replace, their obligations under the Taxonomy.
Furthermore, the FAQs highlight how data from ESRS reports can often serve as a foundational resource for demonstrating Taxonomy alignment, particularly for metrics like greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions or biodiversity impacts. However, meeting the Taxonomy’s stringent performance thresholds requires additional, activity-specific evidence beyond what is typically required under ESRS.
**3. DNSH Criteria Simplified:**
The “Do No Significant Harm” (DNSH) principle is a cornerstone of the EU Taxonomy, requiring that aligned activities avoid undermining other environmental objectives. The FAQs offer deeper insights into how businesses can practically apply this principle, with guidance tailored to address frequent areas of concern such as pollution control, biodiversity protection, and adaptation to climate change.
For instance, when reporting on pollution prevention, the FAQs emphasize the importance of adhering to Best Available Techniques (BAT) as defined in EU regulations. Similarly, for biodiversity, companies must demonstrate that their activities do not negatively affect protected areas or species, often requiring detailed impact assessments.
This clarification helps organizations navigate the DNSH requirements more effectively, ensuring that their activities not only contribute to one environmental goal but also align holistically with the broader sustainability agenda. Importantly, the FAQs recognize the challenges of balancing DNSH compliance with operational realities, offering case-specific examples to guide implementation.
**4. Role of Third-Party Verification:**
Independent verification is critical to the credibility of Taxonomy-aligned reporting. The FAQs address this by detailing when and how third-party verification should occur. This includes defining the frequency of reviews, which varies depending on the nature of the activity.
For example, high-impact activities like renewable energy generation may require annual verification to maintain transparency and trust, while activities with longer time horizons, such as forest management, might necessitate reviews every 10 years. The FAQs also underscore that any material changes to an activity’s environmental impact—such as shifts in production processes or supply chains—should trigger a new verification, even if it falls outside the regular cycle.
Importantly, the FAQs specify that third-party verifiers must be truly independent, ruling out the use of in-house teams or affiliated entities. This ensures that verification processes remain unbiased, safeguarding the integrity of reported data.
**5. Clarification of Key Terms and Scope:**
To address longstanding concerns over interpretational inconsistencies, the FAQs standardize critical terms and phrases across the Delegated Acts. This reduces the subjectivity often associated with assessing Taxonomy alignment and ensures a more uniform application of the framework.
For example, terms like “substantial contribution” and “sustainable use” are now more precisely defined, offering clearer benchmarks for compliance. Additionally, the FAQs provide detailed explanations for activity-specific requirements, such as the metrics used to assess lifecycle GHG emissions or the thresholds for energy efficiency improvements.
By eliminating ambiguity, these clarifications not only simplify the compliance process but also enhance the comparability of Taxonomy-aligned activities across sectors and regions. This is particularly valuable for investors seeking consistent and reliable information to guide sustainable investment decisions.
#### What This Means for Stakeholders
**For Businesses:** The FAQs serve as a critical resource for businesses, simplifying the complex process of aligning their activities with the EU Taxonomy framework. By providing clear guidelines on activity eligibility, reporting requirements, and compliance criteria, the FAQs reduce the ambiguity that has historically surrounded Taxonomy reporting.
This clarity translates into significant benefits for companies. It helps them identify whether their activities meet the technical screening criteria (TSC) and the "Do No Significant Harm" (DNSH) requirements, thereby reducing the risk of regulatory non-compliance. Additionally, the streamlined guidance allows businesses to align their sustainability efforts with broader EU reporting obligations under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) and the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS).
Moreover, the emphasis on third-party verification and standardized terms enhances the credibility of Taxonomy-aligned disclosures, bolstering trust with stakeholders and enabling companies to better articulate their sustainability commitments. This is particularly valuable for businesses seeking to attract ESG-focused investors or secure green financing opportunities.
**For Investors:** The FAQs provide much-needed clarity for investors navigating the growing landscape of sustainable finance. By standardizing reporting practices and criteria, the FAQs enhance the transparency and comparability of Taxonomy-aligned activities, making it easier for investors to assess the environmental impact and financial sustainability of their portfolios.
This clarity strengthens confidence in the reliability of Taxonomy-aligned investments, addressing concerns over greenwashing and inconsistent disclosures. Investors can now make more informed decisions, supported by verifiable data and independent third-party assurance processes.
Furthermore, the FAQs underscore the Taxonomy’s role as a tool for aligning investment strategies with the EU’s broader environmental goals, including climate neutrality and biodiversity preservation. This alignment not only supports ethical investment objectives but also positions investors to capitalize on emerging opportunities in green technologies and sustainable business models.
**For Policymakers and Advisors:** The FAQs represent a roadmap for policymakers and advisors striving to support organizations in their sustainability journeys. By addressing common challenges and ambiguities, the FAQs ensure a consistent application of the Taxonomy framework across industries and regions, aligning practices with Europe’s ambitious environmental objectives.
For policymakers, the detailed guidance facilitates the monitoring and enforcement of compliance, creating a more robust regulatory environment for sustainable finance. It also provides a foundation for refining future policy initiatives, leveraging feedback from stakeholders and market participants to enhance the framework’s effectiveness.
Advisors, including consultants and auditors, benefit from the FAQs’ granular details on technical requirements, verification processes, and activity-specific criteria. This enables them to provide targeted, high-quality support to businesses and investors, helping them navigate the complexities of Taxonomy implementation.
#### What’s New and Noteworthy?
**1. Comparative Lifecycle Assessments**
One of the most significant additions in the FAQs is the guidance on conducting comparative lifecycle greenhouse gas (GHG) emission assessments. These assessments are critical for determining the environmental impact of various economic activities, ensuring they align with the EU Taxonomy’s rigorous criteria for sustainability.
The FAQs provide clear instructions on defining system boundaries and selecting reference scenarios, emphasizing the importance of consistency and transparency. They also highlight the role of sensitivity analysis in understanding how key assumptions may affect results. This guidance ensures that lifecycle assessments across different sectors are comparable, enabling stakeholders to evaluate activities on a level playing field.
This clarity is particularly beneficial for industries with complex supply chains or innovative technologies, where emissions assessments can vary widely depending on methodologies. The standardized approach outlined in the FAQs reduces subjectivity and promotes trust in the reported data.
**2. Sector-Specific Details**
The FAQs go beyond generic guidance by offering tailored answers for specific sectors, including manufacturing, energy, transport, and real estate. This sectoral granularity is crucial because each industry faces unique challenges in aligning with the EU Taxonomy’s technical screening criteria (TSC) and “Do No Significant Harm” (DNSH) requirements.
For example, in manufacturing, the FAQs clarify how components critical to the functionality of sustainable products—like compressors in energy-efficient equipment—can qualify for Taxonomy alignment. In the energy sector, they address questions about the use of renewable energy technologies and the power density requirements for hydropower projects. For real estate, the guidance elaborates on criteria for energy-efficient buildings and renovations, providing actionable insights for property developers and managers.
This level of detail enables businesses within these sectors to identify opportunities for Taxonomy alignment and implement the necessary measures to comply with the framework.
**3. Collaboration and Stakeholder Engagement**
The FAQs underscore the collaborative effort behind their development, reflecting extensive input from market participants, industry groups, and policymakers. Many of the clarifications provided in the document were shaped by feedback received through the European Commission’s stakeholder consultation mechanisms.
This inclusive approach highlights the EU’s commitment to making the Taxonomy framework more user-friendly and responsive to real-world challenges. By incorporating the perspectives of those directly impacted by the regulations, the Commission ensures that the Taxonomy remains practical, relevant, and effective in promoting sustainable economic activities.
Additionally, the FAQs emphasize the role of the EU Platform on Sustainable Finance in facilitating this collaboration. This body acts as a bridge between the Commission and market participants, ensuring that the regulatory framework evolves in line with the needs of stakeholders while staying true to Europe’s environmental ambitions.
