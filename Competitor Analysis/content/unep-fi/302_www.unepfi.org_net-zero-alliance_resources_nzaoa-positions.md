# NZAOA Positions
All signatories of the Net-Zero Asset Owner Alliance (NZAOA) commitment have committed to achieving net-zero greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in their portfolios by 2050, while also emphasizing emissions reduction in the real economy. To meet their individual commitment, members consult [Alliance advisers](https://www.unepfi.org/net-zero-alliance/partners/) to create internal expectations for signatoriesand external expectations for corporates, asset managers, policymakers, and other stakeholders.
### Formulating internal and external expectations through positions
Guidelines for signatories are encompassed in two types of documents:
  * **the[Target Setting Protocol](https://www.unepfi.org/industries/target-setting-protocol-fourth-edition/) (TSP)**, which provides the framework for signatories to set near-term, portfolio-wide targets, and
  * **the NZAOA positions** , which express perspective on specific and focused topics that are central to the fulfillment of the commitment.


The TSP and the positions both represent binding approaches of NZAOA, as stipulated by the [Governance Document.](https://www.unepfi.org/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/NZAOA-Governance-Document_Feb-23.pdf) Regarding positions, NZAOA’s governance embeds a time-bound “comply or explain” expectation for signatories. This means that, informed by NZAOA’s Position, members are required to “where applicable, [adopt or publish] a corresponding individual investment policy or approach”. If a signatory cannot develop a position within 12 months, it is required to explain why that is the case.
### List of Alliance positions
##### [![](https://www.unepfi.org/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/NZAOA-Governmental-Carbon-Pricing_thumbnail.png)](https://www.unepfi.org/industries/net-zero-asset-owner-alliances-updated-position-on-governmental-carbon-pricing/)
##### [Updated Position on Governmental Carbon Pricing](https://www.unepfi.org/industries/net-zero-asset-owner-alliances-updated-position-on-governmental-carbon-pricing/)
Building on its original 2022 position, NZAOA uses new data on the spread, coverage, and effectiveness of carbon pricing instruments globally toreiterates its call to policymakers—follow through on your Paris Agreement commitments and integrate carbon pricing into your climate policy toolkits, following these five design principles:
  * Ensure appropriate coverage and ambition
  * Deliver a just transition
  * Provide a predictable price signal
  * Minimise competitive distortions, and 
  * Promote international cooperation.


##### [![](https://www.unepfi.org/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/NZAOA-Position-on-the-Oil-and-Gas-Sector_cover.jpg)](https://www.unepfi.org/industries/position-on-oil-and-gas-sector/)
##### [Position on the Oil and Gas Sector](https://www.unepfi.org/industries/position-on-oil-and-gas-sector/)
This NZAOA Position on the Oil and Gas Sector underscores the stance that it is necessary for the global economy to transition away from activities that contribute to climate change, such as the combustion of oil and gas.
Recognising that numerous challenges of transitioning to a low-carbon economy can only be mitigated by a concerted effort of the key stakeholders, the Position is expressed in the form of expectations for investors, companies, and policymakers.
In total, the position covers over 20 expectation, which are also summarised in the [Q&A document](https://www.unepfi.org/net-zero-alliance/resources/qa-on-the-nzaoa-position-on-the-oil-and-gas-sector/).
[![](https://www.unepfi.org/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Position-on-Coronavirus-Recovery.png)](https://www.unepfi.org/publications/alliance-position-on-the-coronavirus-recovery/)
##### [Position on the Coronavirus Recovery](https://www.unepfi.org/publications/alliance-position-on-the-coronavirus-recovery/)
NZAOA recognized that the recovery planning from the coronavirus pandemic is a historic opportunity to get back on track with climate goals and accelerate the transition to a zero-carbon economy by 2050. 
These government spending and recovery packages must not only be effective in restarting the economy but also in creating a sustainable economy of the future, which includes the imperative to reduce emissions and uphold the Paris Agreement. The alternative, implementing spending programs without 1.5°C-aligned policies, means potentially committing economies to an unsustainable path that will constrain future efforts to change course.
[![](https://www.unepfi.org/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Thermal-Coal-Position.png)](https://www.unepfi.org/publications/alliance-thermal-coal-position/)
##### [Thermal Coal Position](https://www.unepfi.org/publications/alliance-thermal-coal-position/)
This position solidifies NZAOA’s Commitment, especially in the context of thermal coal, to support corporate action and ambitious public policy to promote rapid decarbonization of developed and emerging economies in a socially responsible manner.
