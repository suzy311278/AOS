Operational Boundaries

20 min read menu_book Source: GHG Protocol Corporate Standard, Chapter 4

After a company has determined its organizational boundaries in terms of the operations that it owns or controls, it then sets its operational boundaries. This involves identifying emissions associated with its operations, categorizing them as direct and indirect emissions, and choosing the scope of accounting and reporting for indirect emissions.

Scope Definition Typical Examples
Scope 1 Direct GHG emissions that occur from sources that are owned or controlled by the company. Boilers, furnaces, company-owned vehicles.
Scope 2 GHG emissions from the generation of purchased electricity, heat, or steam consumed by the company. Purchased electricity grid, heating.
Scope 3 Optional reporting category that allows for the treatment of all other indirect emissions. Supply chain, business travel, waste.
lightbulb

Key Takeaway: Scope Determination

Operational boundaries are not static. As your control over assets shifts (e.g., transitioning from owned vehicle fleets to leased logistics), your scope categorization will shift from Scope 1 to Scope 3.

swap_horiz

The Audit Analogy

Think of scopes like a financial audit. Scope 1 is your direct wallet, Scope 2 is your monthly utility bill, and Scope 3 is the carbon footprint of every person you did business with during the year.

Establishing these boundaries is critical for preventing "double counting" of emissions across different organizations' reports, ensuring that the same emission isn't claimed twice in the same scope by different entities.

Aggregate Calculation Formula

Total GHG = ΣS1 + ΣS2 + ΣS3

Case Study: Manufacturing

A smartphone manufacturer accounts for the diesel burned in their factory backup generators as Scope 1. The electricity they buy to power the assembly line is Scope 2. The emissions from mining the rare earth minerals in the phones, managed by suppliers, is Scope 3.

Checkpoint Quiz

Which scope covers emissions from purchased electricity?

Correct! Scope 2 accounts for indirect GHG emissions from the generation of purchased electricity, heat, or steam consumed by the reporting company.
