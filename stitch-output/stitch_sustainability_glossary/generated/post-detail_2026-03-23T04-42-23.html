ESG Ratings Divergence: Why Two Agencies Can Look at the Same Company Differently

I’ve been diving deep into the technical nuances of ESG reporting lately, and one thing keeps coming up: the sheer lack of correlation between major rating providers. Unlike credit ratings, where S&P and Moody's align about 99% of the time, ESG ratings for the same company can look like they’re describing two different planets.

The "divergence" isn't just a rounding error; it’s fundamental. One agency might weight carbon emissions as 40% of their "E" pillar, while another focuses heavily on resource efficiency. I spent the morning mapping these methodology gaps, and the results are frankly startling for anyone relying on these scores for capital allocation decisions.

My take is that we’re moving toward a world where raw data ownership matters more than the proprietary score itself. If you can't see the underlying metrics, you're essentially outsourcing your investment thesis to an opaque algorithm. We need transparency, not just a letter grade.
