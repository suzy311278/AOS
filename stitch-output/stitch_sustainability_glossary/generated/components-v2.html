Module 04: Soil Carbon Sequestration

Quantifying Soil Health

Understanding the mathematical foundations of Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) stocks and their role in global sustainability metrics.

SOC stock = SOC conc. × Bulk Density × Depth × Coarse Frag.

SOC concentration

Measured as grams of carbon per kilogram of soil (g/kg). It indicates the organic richness of the sample.

Bulk Density

Dry weight of soil per unit volume (g/cm³). Essential for converting concentration to mass.

Sampling Depth

The vertical layer of soil being analyzed (cm), typically the top 30cm for agricultural standards.

Coarse Fragments

Adjustment for stones or gravel larger than 2mm, which do not store organic carbon.

SOC stock = 18 g/kg x 1.35 g/cm3 x 30 cm x 0.1 = 72.9 tC/ha

lightbulb Key Takeaway

SOC stock calculation is the standard metric used by the IPCC to report national greenhouse gas inventories. Without bulk density measurements, simple concentration data can be highly misleading about total carbon storage.

compare Analogy: The Sponge

Think of SOC concentration as the flavor of a tea bag, and Bulk Density as how many tea bags you pack into a box. To know the total "flavor capacity" (SOC Stock) of the box, you must know both the strength of each bag and how tightly they are packed.

Worked Example: Temperate Grassland

A researcher samples a paddock and finds a SOC concentration of 25 g/kg. The bulk density is 1.2 g/cm³. Calculate the SOC stock for the first 10cm depth, assuming no coarse fragments (1.0 factor).

Stock = 25 x 1.2 x 10 x 0.1 = 30.0 tC/ha

While the Walkley-Black method was once the global standard, dry combustion (Dumas method) is now preferred for its precision. High-end spectral analysis (Mid-Infrared Spectroscopy) is emerging as a rapid, non-destructive alternative for large-scale archive data collection.
