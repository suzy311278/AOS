Quantifying Soil Health

Soil organic carbon (SOC) is the largest terrestrial carbon pool, storing roughly 1,500 gigatonnes of carbon in the top metre alone. Accurately quantifying SOC stocks is fundamental to any land-based carbon crediting methodology, because it determines both the baseline carbon inventory and the magnitude of sequestration that management changes can achieve.

SOC Stock Equation

SOCstock = SOCconc × BD × D × CF

SOCconc - SOC Concentration

The mass of organic carbon per unit mass of dry soil, typically measured in g/kg. Determined through laboratory analysis of soil samples via dry combustion or wet oxidation.

BD - Bulk Density

The dry mass of soil per unit volume (g/cm³), reflecting soil compaction. Collected using a core sampling method to preserve the natural soil structure.

D - Sampling Depth

The vertical depth of the soil layer being measured, in centimetres. IPCC Tier 2 methods typically require sampling to at least 30 cm depth.

CF - Coarse Fragment Factor

A correction factor (0 to 1) that accounts for the volume occupied by stones, gravel, and roots larger than 2 mm, which do not contain organic carbon.

Worked Calculation

SOCstock = 18 g/kg × 1.35 g/cm³ × 30 cm × 0.1 = 72.9 tC/ha
lightbulb

Key Takeaway

The SOC stock calculation is the IPCC-standard method for converting field measurements into tonnes of carbon per hectare. All four input variables must be measured or estimated for each soil stratum; omitting the coarse fragment correction is a common error that overestimates carbon stocks by 5 to 30% in rocky soils.

compare

Analogy: The Sponge

Think of soil as a kitchen sponge. The SOC concentration is how much tea the sponge has absorbed per gram of sponge material. Bulk density is how tightly you squeeze the sponge: a compressed sponge holds less liquid per unit volume. Depth is how thick the sponge is. And the coarse fragment factor accounts for any marbles lodged inside the sponge that displace volume but absorb nothing.

Worked Example: Temperate Grassland

A temperate grassland site in southern France has an SOC concentration of 25 g/kg, bulk density of 1.2 g/cm³, sampling depth of 10 cm, and a coarse fragment correction of 0.1. Calculate the carbon stock for this stratum.

Stock = 25 × 1.2 × 10 × 0.1 = 30.0 tC/ha

Dry Combustion vs. Wet Oxidation

There are two primary laboratory methods for determining SOC concentration. Dry combustion (the Dumas method) heats the sample to 950 to 1050 degrees Celsius in a pure oxygen atmosphere and measures the CO2 released by infrared detection. It captures total carbon, so inorganic carbonates must be removed first (by acid pre-treatment) or measured separately.

Wet oxidation (the Walkley-Black method) uses potassium dichromate and concentrated sulfuric acid to oxidise organic carbon at lower temperatures. It is cheaper and faster but typically recovers only 76 to 77% of total organic carbon, requiring a correction factor of 1.3. The IPCC recommends dry combustion for Tier 2 and Tier 3 assessments because of its higher precision and completeness.

In practice, many national soil inventories still rely on Walkley-Black data for cost reasons, applying laboratory-specific recovery corrections. When comparing SOC stocks across studies, always verify which analytical method was used and whether a correction factor has been applied.
